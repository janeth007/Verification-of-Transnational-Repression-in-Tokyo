
# Investigative Case Brief  
## Digital Verification of Suspected Transnational Repression in Tokyo

**Project Lead:** Janet Zheng Huang 

**Focus Area:** Digital Verification · Transnational Repression · Japan  

**Location of Analysis:** Shinjuku Station (South Exit), Tokyo

---

## Executive Summary

This project demonstrates the application of open-source digital verification methods to document an incident of **suspected monitoring and intimidation of diaspora activists** during a peaceful assembly in Tokyo.

Through structured visual analysis of publicly available digital material, this brief illustrates how verified digital evidence can support **protective engagement** and **policy advocacy** in the Japanese context.

---

## Scope and Simulation Notice

This project is a **methodological demonstration** of open-source verification techniques used in human rights investigations.
 
- No private individuals are identified  
- No allegation of criminal responsibility is made  
- The analysis relies exclusively on open-source information  

The purpose is to demonstrate **investigative process and ethical reasoning**, not to publish sensitive evidence.

---

## Methodology: Investigative Workflow

### A. Digital Discovery & Archiving

**Source Identification**  
Publicly available video footage depicting individuals filming protesters during a peaceful assembly near Shinjuku Station was identified via social media and news platforms.

**Preservation**  
Original posts were archived using the **Wayback Machine** to preserve timestamps and ensure evidentiary continuity.

---

### B. Geolocation (Proving WHERE)

> **Note on Visual Materials:**  
> In a live investigative context, this section would include side-by-side visual comparisons between source footage and Google Street View to demonstrate geolocation.  
> For ethical and privacy reasons, and to avoid reproducing sensitive imagery, visual materials are not displayed in this public repository.

**Tools Used**
- Google Earth Pro  
- Google Street View  

**Process**  
Distinct visual anchor points were identified through frame-by-frame analysis, including:
- The façade and window structure of **Shinjuku L-Tower**
- Street layout and station exit signage consistent with the **South Exit of Shinjuku Station**

These features were cross-referenced with Street View imagery to confirm the filming location.

### C. Chronolocation (Proving WHEN)

**Tool Used**
- SunCalc.org  

**Process**  
Shadow length and direction visible in the footage were analyzed and compared against solar position data for Shinjuku.

**Result**  
The shadow angles were consistent with an early afternoon timeframe (approximately 2:30 PM JST), supporting temporal authenticity.

---

## Data Structuring & Pattern Observation

Verified observations were aggregated into a small-N dataset tracking:
- Presence of sustained filming
- Proximity to demonstrators
- Repetition of similar behavior across assemblies

**Preliminary Observation**  
A recurring pattern of **non-consensual, close-range filming** was observed, consistent with documented transnational repression tactics.

---

## Policy Relevance (Japan / Asia Division)

This verification exercise demonstrates how digital evidence can inform:

- **Protective engagement** by Japanese authorities toward at-risk diaspora communities  
- **Legislative and advocacy discussions** on protections against transnational repression
